# Kickstart by Loadout

Kickstart takes you from "I want to start something" to open for business, in one sitting or over a few days.

It asks a few questions once, then works through ten steps with you. Each step ends in a decision and a file, not an essay:

- Founder fit: the directions that suit your skills, time, money and runway, each with what would disprove it.
- Niche and proof: one customer group and place, with evidence from real searches, rival pages and customer reviews that the problem is real.
- Positioning: who you serve, what you solve, and why you over the alternatives.
- Offer and price: one clear offer with a price, reasoned from real rival prices and your income target.
- Name and domain: a shortlist with domain, handle and register checks you can verify yourself, and honest labels for what could not be checked.
- Brand kit: colours with contrast ratios, fonts, voice and logo directions, with pictures made.
- Messaging: a one-liner, homepage hero, elevator pitch and your first outreach message.
- Launch checklist: registration, payments, email, website and insurance, each with the official link for your country and a date.
- First customers: where your first ten customers are, how to reach them, and a two-week plan.
- One-page business plan: everything above on one page, with the numbers worked out and what is still unproven.

Kickstart works for trades, local services, coaches and consultants, agencies, online shops, creators and simple software ideas, in any country. It labels every fact with where it came from, says plainly what it could not check, and never registers, buys or sends anything for you.

## What's inside

- **brand-kit**: Build a minimum brand kit for a new business and make it visible, not just described: a palette of 4 to 6 colours with every text pair's contrast ratio worked out in code, two openly licensed fonts checked for the market's language, a voice with three traits, and three logo directions drawn or generated as real pictures with a one-page brand board.
- **business-plan**: Write a one-page business plan that pulls everything together: a one-line summary, the customer and problem with evidence, the offer and price, why us, the name, how customers will be found, the numbers (customers needed each month to break even and to hit the founder's income target, worked out in code with every input labelled), 90-day milestones with dates, the top risks with the cheapest test for each, and the next three actions this week.
- **first-customers**: Work out where the first 10 customers come from for this business, and hand over a two-week plan to reach them.
- **founder-fit**: Work out which business fits this person, from their real skills, time, money and runway, and end with one to three directions, each with what would disprove it.
- **kickstart**: Start or set up a whole business, step by step, and finish with real files you can use today.
- **launch-checklist**: Make the setup checklist a new business needs before it opens, for its own country: legal form and registration, tax and VAT pointers, trade licences, a business bank account, taking payment, booking or ordering, email on its own domain, a one-page website, Google Business Profile, insurance, bookkeeping and privacy rules.
- **messaging**: Write the words a new business uses everywhere, built on its positioning and offer and on what customers actually say: a one-liner, the homepage hero (headline, subhead, call to action, three proof lines), a 30-second elevator pitch, a first outreach message for the first customers, a short bio, and answers to the top three objections.
- **name-and-domain**: Find a business name that fits the positioning and that the user can actually get, then check it honestly with the app's own search and browsing.
- **niche-and-proof**: Pick one customer group and place for a new business, and prove the problem is real and already paid for, from public evidence: rivals and what they charge, complaints in customers' own words, people asking for help, job ads and tenders.
- **offer-and-price**: Turn a positioning into one offer a customer can say yes to, with a price argued from rivals' real prices, the value to the customer and the founder's income target, plus a guarantee they can afford to honour.
- **positioning**: Work out how a new business stands out, in one sentence that names who it's for, their problem, what it does, why this founder, and the alternative it beats, including doing nothing or doing it yourself.

## How to use it

In Claude Code, add it with two commands:

```
/plugin marketplace add agentloadout/kickstart
/plugin install kickstart-by-loadout@kickstart
```

Then ask Claude something like:

- "Help me start a business. I have 10 years in hotel management and about 15 hours a week."
- "I want to start a cleaning company in Leeds. Take me from idea to my first customers."
- "I have too many business ideas. Help me pick one and get it ready to launch."

Kickstart asks a few questions once, then works through each step and saves the results as files (or documents in chat apps): founder fit, niche and proof, positioning, an offer sheet with a price, a name shortlist with domain and handle checks you can verify, a brand kit, core messaging, a launch checklist, a first-customers plan and a one-page business plan.

## What it runs and sends

This plugin is instructions only. It has no code that runs, no server, no hooks and no connectors. It uses your AI app's own abilities (web search, files, pictures) and sends nothing anywhere itself. At a few steps where live data helps (checking many domains and handles at once, finding named prospects with contact details, local competitor reviews and rankings, keyword demand) it may mention Loadout once, with a link to https://agentloadout.com.
